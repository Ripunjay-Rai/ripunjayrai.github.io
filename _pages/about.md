 About Me
</h1>

I am Ripunjay Rai, a physicist specializing in computational materials science, first-principles simulations, and atomistic modeling. I completed my M.Sc. in Physics at the Central University of Punjab, with a CGPA of 7.95/10. My research interests lie at the intersection of condensed matter physics, computational modeling, and machine learning.

I am currently working at the Centre for Nano and Material Sciences (CNMS), Jain (Deemed-to-be University), Bengaluru, India, where my research involves the development of machine-learning interatomic potentials for metal clusters. My broader research interests include combining first-principles calculations with machine-learning techniques to investigate atomic interactions, structural stability, and the properties of complex materials.

Current Research

My current research focuses on machine-learning interatomic potentials for metal clusters. These potentials aim to provide computationally efficient representations of atomic interactions, enabling atomistic simulations of systems that may be computationally demanding for direct first-principles methods. I am interested in developing reliable computational models informed by density functional theory (DFT) and evaluating their ability to describe the structural and energetic properties of atomistic systems.

Research Interests
Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials: Development and application of machine-learning models for atomistic simulations of metal clusters.
Density Functional Theory: First-principles calculations of structural stability, electronic structure, and energetic properties of materials.

Van der Waals Heterostructures: Study of interfacial interactions and electronic properties of layered materials.
Electronic Structure Theory: Analysis of band structures, density of states, charge distributions, and Fermi surface topology.
Previous Research

During my M.Sc. dissertation, I investigated two-dimensional materials and van der Waals heterostructures for energy-storage applications using first-principles calculations. My work focused on structural stability, electronic properties, charge-density distributions, and quantum capacitance to assess the potential of transition-metal-based materials for supercapacitor electrodes.

I primarily used Quantum ESPRESSO for DFT calculations and VESTA for structural visualization. This research established my foundation in computational condensed matter physics and strengthened my interest in atomistic simulations and computational materials design.
